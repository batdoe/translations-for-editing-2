DOSSIER — SWEDISH (Svenska)
Spine & Spells / Bookbinding paranormal cozy mystery series.
Read with TRANSLATOR_CORE.txt — the completeness mandate and "do not improve" rule bind here too.
Built to MATCH the existing published Swedish editions of this series (Books 1–3) — keep their
conventions so the whole series reads consistently.

NARRATIVE TENSE (LOCKED — must be IDENTICAL across every chapter of a book)
- First mirror the SOURCE: if the book narrates in the present, narrate in the present; if in the
  past (the usual case), narrate in the past. Never change a book's basic tense.
- For PAST-TENSE narration, use the tense named below and keep it identical in EVERY chapter.
  Mixing narrative tenses across chapters is a visible continuity error a reader WILL notice.
- SWEDISH: preteritum ('jag gick') for narration. Do NOT narrate in perfekt ('jag har gått').

REGISTER & VOICE
- Colloquial contemporary Swedish; polished but never stiff — an educated, witty everyday voice.
- Natural Swedish syntax and V2 word order; use it for emphasis as a Swedish writer would, not as
  English dictates. Avoid English calques and Anglicisms — if a phrase "sounds translated," replace
  it with the idiomatic Swedish equivalent.
- Warmth comes from word choice and rhythm, not diminutives (Swedish has few). Cozy-calibration words:
  mysig / gosig (snug, cozy), lagom, hemtrevlig. If a scene feels mysig, the translation is on track.

HUMOR
- Dry Swedish understatement (lagom-flavoured); self-deprecation for the protagonist. Irony over
  slapstick. The paranormal gets a matter-of-fact shrug, like bad November weather — not horror.

DIALOGUE PUNCTUATION (critical — follow exactly)
- HOUSE STANDARD (ruled 2026-09-04 by Lisa — SUPERSEDES the 2026-09-03 citattecken ruling):
  STRAIGHT DOUBLE QUOTES "..." (U+0022 at both ends) for dialogue. This is what the published
  Spine and Spells Swedish editions actually use (Book 1: 1,000+ spans; Book 4: 1,710), and the
  corpus is not to be retrofitted to the 99-99 form.
  NOT the 99-99 citattecken (”…”), NOT the English 66-99 pair (“…”), NOT talstreck (—).
    EN: She said, "Good morning."               ->  "God morgon," sa hon.
    EN: "Where are you going?" she asked.       ->  "Vart är du på väg?" frågade hon.
    EN: "I don't know," he said. "It's strange." ->  "Jag vet inte," sa han. "Det är konstigt."
  Attribution verb stays lower-case after the comma ("sa hon", "frågade hon", "sa jag").
  NB Book 5 Dutch and some sibling editions use curly marks; that is per-language, not a reason
  to change Swedish. Never mix two dialogue systems inside one book.
- Quoted words inside narration: also "...".
- Nested quote (a quote inside dialogue): single ’…’ (U+2019 at both ends, same 99-99 logic).
- Question mark ? and exclamation ! as in English. Ellipsis … (single character). Em dash — for an
  interruption or aside, with spaces.
- Never begin a narration-only paragraph with a dialogue quote that isn’t actually speech.
